Chapter 8Lightning round ⚡

Six fair questions, answered fast

🤔Why not FP8?

At 71 GB it fits on one H200 too, Hopper has FP8 tensor cores, and Red Hat's FP8 build of this model keeps 99.9% of the BF16 score. It's the first variant I'd add.

FP8 on 1 GPU: __ tok/s

🐢Why did spec decode lose?

That run had enforce_eager on, which in vLLM 0.18 turns off torch.compile and CUDA graphs, and the draft makes five small passes for every 70B pass.

tokens per 70B pass: __ · no-eager: __ tok/s

📏Are 20 requests enough?

For one-at-a-time speed it's a sanity check, and it lines up with Red Hat's own H100 numbers. Cost per token comes from the load test at concurrency 1 to 64.

tok/s per GPU at c=32: __

🐣Does it hold for an 8B?

Smaller models lose more. In Red Hat's study INT4 kept 97.4% of the 70B's score on OpenLLM v2 and 96.1% of the 8B's, so check an 8B carefully.

Kurtic et al., ACL 2025

🧰Why AWQ, not GPTQ or torchao?

All three end up as checkpoints vLLM serves. Red Hat's paper found GPTQ slightly ahead on harder benchmarks, so it's the next INT4 variant to try.

vLLM 0.18 serves all three

🔀Can I route per request?

Yes. Put the variants behind one Gateway and route on model name, and llm-d's scheduler picks the replica already holding your prefix in KV cache.

05 — WHAT THE DATA ACTUALLY SAYS

Three things that surprised me 🤯

  • INT4 is the value play. It gets 44 tok/s on one GPU compared to FP16's 47 tok/s on two GPUs. So you're getting nearly identical throughput at a third of the GPU cost.
  • FP16 is the fastest. Not INT4. Tensor parallelism across 2× H200s gives you more raw compute bandwidth than a single quantized GPU. Nobody guesses this 🤷
  • Speculative decode is the slowest. 40 tok/s. The enforce_eager flag disables CUDA graph optimizations in vLLM 0.18, so the theory is sound but the tooling isn't there yet.

Benchmarked with 20 requests, avg 220+ tokens/response · Llama 3.1 70B Instruct

06 — BUT SPEED ISN'T THE WHOLE STORY

Same prompt, three different answers 🐑🐑🐑

A farmer has 17 sheep. All but 9 run away. How many sheep does the farmer have left?

FP16  ✓ CORRECT

The farmer has 9 sheep left.

"All but 9" means every sheep
except 9 ran away. So 17 - 9 = 8
sheep ran away, leaving 9 behind.

The tricky part is that "all but 9"
means 9 remain, not that 9 left.

INT4  ✗ WRONG

The farmer has 8 sheep left.

Step 1: Start with 17 sheep.
Step 2: 9 sheep run away.
Step 3: 17 - 9 = 8 sheep remain.

The answer is 8 sheep.

Spec Decode  ✓ CORRECT

The farmer has 9 sheep left.

"All but 9 run away" means every
sheep except 9 ran away. So 17 - 9
= 8 sheep ran away, leaving 9.

"All but 9" describes how many
remain, not how many left.

INT4 confidently gets it wrong, and that's not a performance metric, that's a support ticket 📩

07 — QUESTIONS I CAN SEE FORMING

Before you ask 😏

"Why not FP8? That's the real sweet spot."

Fair point. FP8 gives you most of FP16 quality with better memory usage. We tested the extremes on purpose because the boundaries of the tradeoff are where the interesting decisions live. FP8 is a great middle ground for a lot of workloads.

"Can you just route to the right variant per request?"

Yes, and that's exactly the play. Run INT4 for your chatbot traffic, FP16 for your reasoning workloads, on the same cluster. That's what OpenShift AI's model serving is built for, routing requests to the right variant based on the workload.

"Is this just a Llama 70B thing?"

The relative tradeoffs hold across models, but smaller models actually quantize better because there's more redundancy per parameter. A 7B model at INT4 loses less quality than 70B at INT4. These benchmarks are Llama 70B because that's what people actually deploy at scale.

"When will spec decode actually be fast?"

When vLLM ships CUDA graph support for speculative decoding, which is active development. The technique itself is proven, but enforce_eager disables the optimizations that make vLLM fast in the first place. We showed it honestly because you deserve real data.

08 — THE CHEAT SHEET 📋

Quantization strategy is an infrastructure decision and the right answer depends on your workload.

Use INT4 when... 🟡

Cost is king. Your workload is chatbots, content generation, classification, or summarization at scale, and you can tolerate edge-case quality loss.

1 GPU · 38 GB · 44 tok/s

Use FP16 when... 🔵

A wrong answer has consequences. Safety-critical apps, complex reasoning, code generation, anything where "close enough" isn't good enough.

2 GPUs · 260 GB · 47 tok/s

Use Spec Decode when... 🟣

You need FP16 quality on interactive workloads and can wait for vLLM to ship CUDA graph support. The technique works, the tooling is catching up.

2 GPUs · 260 GB · 40 tok/s
